<summary>[2] Behavioral and hypothetical interviews</summary>

#### Recap:  
Behavioral Interview는 과거에 실제로 어떻게 행동했는지를 통해 역량과 문제 해결방식을 평가하며 STAR 형식을 따라 대답 연습을 하는 게 좋다. 그리고 Hypothetical Interview의 경우는 실제로 닥치지 않은 문제에 대해 사고 과정과 접근법을 평가하며 정답보다는 논리적 사고 흐름을 중점적으로 본다.

#### Behavioral interview   
Behavioral questions usually start with phrases such as “Tell me about a time when” or “Give me an example of” or “Describe a decision you made.” Interviewers are looking for examples of what you have done and how you have done it. They may follow up with more probing questions such as, “What did you do then?” or “What was the result?” Using a behavioral interview technique like the STAR approach can help structure your answers.

[S]ituation:  Describe the situation you were in or the task you needed to accomplish  
[T]ask: Explain the goal you were working toward  
[A]ctivity: Detail the specific steps you took and the role(s) you played  
[R]esult: Describe your accomplishments and the overall outcome  

#### Hypothetical questions  
Questions often begin with “Imagine that…” and are designed to assess your thought process rather than “right” or “wrong” solutions. We don’t use brain teasers, but instead want to know how you would approach a typical problem scenario that is related to the role or that Googlers have faced in the past.  

A strong answer will illustrate or include:  
- An understanding of the problem:  
Did you display a general understanding of the question asked by outlining what experiences and factors are relevant to solve the problem and what additional work needs to be done?

- Thoughtful problem solving:  
Are you gathering information in order to address specific factors or pulling from specific experience? Are you thinking about how to gather information or conduct research and how to use that information to solve the problem? Are you getting to the root cause?

- Potential solutions:  
Does your solution answer the initial question or solve the initial problem? Are you considering and appropriately weighing the pros and cons of your solution?

- Support for your solutions:  
Are you providing rationale for why a certain solution is best, despite previously stated pros and cons? Did you describe potential success metrics to support your solution?

- Strong communication:  
Are your responses structured and logical? Do you balance brevity and detail?

#### Recap:
전적으로 동의하지만 참 쉽지 않은 일들. System Architecture를 잘 선택하고 단순 Coder가 되는 게 아니라 전체적 과정을 이해하고 장점, 단점 또는 특징들을 잘 설명할 수 있어야 한다. 이런 과정을 거치면 당연히 테스팅과 디버깅이 가능할 것이라 생각한다. 요즘 일하며 많이 고민에 해당되는 내용의 이야기들이다.

Depending on the role and experience level, you may be expected to demonstrate more expertise in coding or code comprehension, data structures and algorithms, systems architecture or other domain areas.

**Code and system health:**
- Keeping our codebase clean and organized (readable) is important!

**Code comprehension:**  
- Reviewing code is like solving a puzzle - the ability to dive into unfamiliar code, quickly understand its structure, identify underlying problems, and suggest improvements is crucial. 

**Data structures and algoritms:**
- you’ll need to know which data structures and algorithms are the best tools for the job.
- We’ll be looking to you to make smart choices to keep systems running smoothly and efficiently.
- You’ll want to demonstrate strong quantitative skills, including probability and combinatorics, with familiarity in n-choose-k problems.

**Programming:**
- you demonstrate expertise in your chosen language, understanding its idioms and trade-offs.

**Software testing and debugging:**
- It means understanding the different stages of testing, from checking if the product meets requirements to ensuring it performs well under pressure.

**System architecture:**  
- A well-designed system is one that’s efficient, scalable, and easy to test and maintain.

**Interview logistics**
- May be using Google meet

    - usually, asks Coding / Algorithms / System design / Data Structure & Algorithms / Coding design / Sorting and Trees / Big O notations / APIs
- Can't use IDE or Compiler, Practice code on a whiteboard, Google Docs, or Piece of paper


**During interviews**
- problem sovling skills
- coding fluency


**Coding**  
should focus preparation on the following area:  
- 1: proficient coding / how to test code / how to come up with corner and edge cases
- 2: common data structure / how to assess and improve the space and time comp

**Testing**
- Potential Questions:  
    - How would you unit test the code you write?
    - What interesting inputs or test cases can you think of?

**Data structures** 
- NP-complete problems 
    - traveling salesman
    - knapsack
- Arrays / Hash tables / Linked lists / Graphs / Trees / Stack / Priority queues

**Quantitative skills**
- Elementary probability theory
- Combinatorics
- N choose K


**Graph**  
should know graph algorithms:
- Distance
- Search
- Connectivity
- Cycle detection  

- Baisc ways to represent a graph in memory:
    - Objects and pointers
    - Matrix
    - Adjacency list  
    Be sure to familiarize yourself with each representation and its pros and cons.
- Basic graph traversal algorithms:
    - Breadth-first search
    - Depth-first search
    - computational complexity
    - Trade-offs
    - How to implement them in real code

**Recursion**
- Recursion 
- Dynamic Programming

**System design**
- test your ablity to combine knowledge, theory, experience, judgment - shows a real world engineering problem
    - interfaces / distrivuted systems / limitations / robustness / considreing trade-offs
- Operating Systems
    - processes / thread / concurrency issues / locks / mutexes / semaphores / monitors / deadlock & livelock / resources / context switching

**Key approaches**
- Explain and clarify your answers:
    - not just evaluating your technical abilities, and how you approach and try and solve problems. 
- Verbalize and check your assumpition:
    - if you don't understand a problem, or need more information. Many of questions asked in a interview deliberately vague because engineers are looking to see how you engage the problem.
- Think about ways to improve your proposed answer.
    - In many cases, the first solution that springs to mind isn't the most elegant and many need some refining.
    - Talk through your initial thoughts with the interviewer. 
    - Jumping immediately into presenting a brute force solution will be less well-received than trying to take time to compose a more efficeint solution.
- PRACTICE! PRACTICE! PRACTICE!
